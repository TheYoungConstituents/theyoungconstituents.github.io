My Perspective on Digital and Social Media Addiction.

by Avery Williams, October 31 2026

Back to Research & Insights

During this month, we’ve explored a lot about how social media addiction, including how social media influences our brains, habits, and emotional well being. In my opinion, I found this topic very interesting and fun to write about because a lot of the information we talked about was not entirely new to me. I was already aware that social media platforms are designed to keep users engaged, but researching greater definitions allowed me to explore the topic in greater depth.

One thing I've noticed is how something as simple as a notification or a like can influence our behaviour. Social media is not just a form of entertainment, but its design can encourage repeated use through rewards, feedback, and personalized recommendations. I think understanding how this all works is important because it allows us to look beyond our screen time and consider the psychological factors influencing our decisions.

I believe social media addiction is a topic that deserves more attention, especially amongst young people. Adolescence is an important stage of development, and online experiences can influence how we perceive ourselves, interact with others, and respond to social approval. However, I also believe that frequent social media use should not automatically be labelled as addiction. From my perspective there is a clear difference between enjoying a platform and having difficulty when it comes to controlling your amount of use when it begins affecting your everyday life.

Something that stands out to me a lot is that you really have to recognize when your habits are turning into addiction. Social media platforms won't tell you when it's too much or when to stop. These platforms are made for people to continuously scroll and watch without even realizing what they are doing. You have to be aware and understand the issue. While I don't hink the solution is to avoid social media altogether, but to understand how it works and recognize when our use may be becoming unhealthy.

Overall, researching this month’s topic gave me the opportunity to examine and teach others about the relationship between technology, psychology, and behaviour more closely. As someone who gathered and organized the information used throughout our posts, I wanted to go beyond surface level facts and explore the deeper reasons behind the behaviours we were discussing. For me, the most valuable part of this topic was not simply identifying that social media can become addictive, but understanding all the factors hat can contribute to it and why that understanding matters.
